## A concise AST treatment principle
> [!CAUTION]
> Important caution

One important real-world note:

If this question is personal or connected to symptoms happening now, the safest next step is urgent support from a mental health professional or crisis service, because psychosis needs proper clinical assessment.

Conceptually, according to AST, the treatment goal would be to recalibrate the whole affective-survival inference system so that ambiguous signals are no longer given excessive probability, emotional force, survival significance, or catastrophic future weight. In other words, AST would not frame psychosis mainly as “bad thoughts” or only as “too much dopamine,” but as a state in which the organism is over-experiencing weak or ambiguous cues as real, urgent, and survival-relevant. That would make the therapeutic target: reduce misassigned salience, restore evidence-weighting, lower threat loading, and stabilise memory/anticipation loops. This is conceptually consistent with computational psychiatry accounts of psychosis as abnormal inference or aberrant salience.

## Psychosis Treatment Translated into AST Language

Within an AST framework, psychosis can be understood as a state in which the consciousness-generating system becomes miscalibrated, causing ambiguous signals to acquire excessive certainty, emotional force, survival importance, and temporal amplification. The conceptual aim of treatment is therefore not simply to suppress symptoms, but to recalibrate affective-survival inference so that conscious salience becomes proportional to reality again.

Using the AST equation:

C_t=∑_k( A_k×S_k×V_k×P_k)+Ψ_past+Φ_future

Psychosis-like states emerge when several parameters become pathologically amplified or distorted simultaneously. Treatment, conceptually, therefore involves restoring these parameters toward calibrated ranges.

### The Six-Layer AST Treatment Framework

## Layer 1 — Recalibrating P_k: Perceived Probability

Problem in psychosis<br/>
The system assigns excessively high probability to weak or ambiguous evidence.<br/>
Examples:<br/>
“That sound definitely was a threatening voice.” <br/>
“People are certainly watching me.” <br/>
“This coincidence cannot be random.” <br/>

Mathematically:<br/>
P(H_k∣D_t)<br/>
becomes inflated despite insufficient evidence.

#### AST treatment goal

Reduce over-certainty and restore proportional evidence weighting.<br/>
The aim is not necessarily to force the person to abandon beliefs instantly, but to:<br/>
increase tolerance for uncertainty, <br/>
improve alternative interpretation generation, <br/>
weaken rigid posterior certainty. <br/>
Conceptually, treatment lowers:<br/>
P_k↓<br/>
toward a value more consistent with actual evidence.<br/>

Clinical parallels<br/>
CBT for psychosis <br/>
reality testing <br/>
reducing paranoid certainty <br/>
grounding techniques <br/>
antipsychotic medication reducing aberrant salience<br/>

## Layer 2 — Recalibrating A_k: Affective Intensity

Problem in psychosis<br/>
Signals are experienced with excessive emotional force.<br/>
A weak cue may feel:<br/>
terrifying, <br/>
overwhelming, <br/>
urgent, <br/>
emotionally intrusive. <br/>
The issue is not only belief, but the felt intensity attached to the experience.<br/>

AST treatment goal<br/>
Reduce the emotional amplification of ambiguous stimuli.<br/>
Conceptually:<br/>
A_k↓<br/>
so that signals no longer dominate consciousness through sheer emotional force.<br/>

Clinical parallels<br/>
reducing stress arousal <br/>
emotional regulation <br/>
sleep stabilization <br/>
calming hypervigilance <br/>
reducing agitation and distress <br/>
trauma-informed stabilization <br/>

## Layer 3 — Recalibrating S_k: Survival Relevance

Problem in psychosis <br/>
Neutral or weak stimuli become loaded with exaggerated personal or survival significance.<br/>
Examples:<br/>
“This message is secretly about me.” <br/>
“This event means I am in danger.” <br/>
“That stranger’s glance has deep significance.” <br/>
In AST terms, the system falsely tags signals as survival-critical.<br/>

AST treatment goal<br/>
Restore accurate relevance assignment.<br/>
The aim is to weaken false threat-loading and reduce inappropriate salience attribution.<br/>
Conceptually:<br/>
S_k↓<br/>
for signals that are not genuinely dangerous or biologically urgent.<br/>

Clinical parallels<br/>
reducing paranoid interpretation <br/>
reducing self-referential thinking <br/>
lowering aberrant salience <br/>
social grounding and contextualization<br/>

## Layer 4 — Recalibrating V_k: Valence

Problem in psychosis<br/>
Valence becomes excessively negative, threatening, persecutory, or distorted.<br/>
Neutral stimuli acquire:<br/>
fear, <br/>
hostility, <br/>
accusation, <br/>
shame, <br/>
catastrophic meaning. <br/>
Sometimes valence may also become pathologically grandiose:<br/>
excessive importance, <br/>
divine significance, <br/>
unrealistic empowerment.<br/>

So:<br/>
V_k<br/>
becomes distorted away from calibrated emotional interpretation.<br/>
AST treatment goal<br/>
Restore proportional emotional meaning.<br/>
The aim is not emotional flattening, but reducing distorted negative or grandiose valuation.<br/>
Conceptually:<br/>
V_k→"balanced range" <br/>

Clinical parallels<br/>
reducing persecutory interpretation <br/>
trauma therapy <br/>
emotional processing <br/>
compassionate reframing <br/>
social reconnection <br/>
affect regulation<br/>

## Layer 5 — Recalibrating Ψ_past: Past/Memory Weighting

Problem in psychosis <br/>
Past fear, trauma, humiliation, or learned threat excessively dominate present interpretation.<br/>
The present becomes filtered through old survival memories.<br/>
Examples:<br/>
previous bullying shaping paranoia, <br/>
trauma shaping threat anticipation, <br/>
fear memories amplifying neutral cues.<br/>

So:

Ψ_past<br/>
becomes overweighted.<br/>
AST treatment goal<br/>
Reduce maladaptive memory dominance over present inference.<br/>
Conceptually:<br/>
Ψ_past↓<br/>
so that current evidence matters more than traumatic priors.<br/>

Clinical parallels<br/>
trauma-informed therapy <br/>
CBT <br/>
EMDR-related approaches <br/>
rebuilding safe experiences <br/>
attachment repair <br/>
social stability<br/>

## Layer 6 — Recalibrating Φ_future: Anticipatory Weighting

Problem in psychosis <br/>
The system catastrophically over-predicts future harm or significance.<br/>
Examples:<br/>
“Something terrible is about to happen.” <br/>
“This is escalating.” <br/>
“I am being targeted.” <br/>
“The world is changing around me.” <br/>
Future prediction becomes threat-amplified.<br/>

So:<br/>
Φ_future<br/>
becomes excessively large.<br/>
AST treatment goal<br/>
Reduce catastrophic anticipation and restore future uncertainty tolerance.<br/>
Conceptually:<br/>
Φ_future↓<br/>
so that anticipated futures no longer dominate present consciousness.<br/>

Clinical parallels<br/>
anxiety reduction <br/>
grounding into present sensory reality <br/>
sleep restoration <br/>
reducing doom anticipation <br/>
stress stabilization <br/>
supportive routines and predictability<br/>


### System-Level Interpretation

The strongest AST interpretation is that psychosis is rarely caused by one parameter alone.<br/>
Instead, multiple parameters become simultaneously amplified:<br/>
P_k↑,A_k↑,S_k↑,V_k " distorted",Ψ_past↑,Φ_future↑<br/>

The result is:<br/>
C_t↑↑

meaning consciousness becomes dominated by highly salient internally generated threat interpretations.<br/>
Treatment therefore aims to bring the system back toward calibrated conscious inference:<br/>

P_k→"calibrated" <br/>
A_k→"regulated" <br/>
S_k→"proportional"<br/>
V_k→"balanced"<br/>
Ψ_past→"integrated" <br/>
Φ_future→"stable" <br/>

### Dopamine in AST Terms

Within AST, dopamine dysregulation may be interpreted as one biological mechanism that inflates:<br/>
P_k(certainty), <br/>
S_k(salience), <br/>
and potentially A_k(motivational/emotional force). <br/>

This maps closely onto the aberrant salience hypothesis.<br/>
However, AST broadens the framework by arguing that conscious suffering also depends on:<br/>
emotional valuation, <br/>
memory loading, <br/>
future anticipation, <br/>
and survival interpretation. <br/>

Thus, dopamine dysregulation may initiate or amplify the process, but the full conscious experience emerges from the interaction of all AST parameters.


#### Conclusion

Within Affective Survival Theory, the conceptual treatment target in psychosis is not merely symptom suppression, but recalibration of the affective-survival inference system. Psychotic experience arises when ambiguous or weak cues acquire excessive posterior credibility, excessive affective intensity, inflated survival relevance, and amplified temporal loading from past experience and anticipated future harm. On this view, treatment aims to reduce false salience assignment, restore proportional evidence-weighting, lower maladaptive threat valuation, and dampen catastrophic memory–anticipation loops. Dopamine dysregulation may be interpreted as one important biological mechanism contributing to this miscalibration, particularly through aberrant salience assignment, but AST suggests that a full account must extend beyond dopamine alone to include affective, temporal, and survival-based dimensions of conscious inference.
