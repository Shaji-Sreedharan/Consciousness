# From Natural Selection as an Automatic Control System to Bayesian Selection to The AST Consciousness Model


## 1. Natural selection as an automatic control system

The original intuition is:

Natural selection works like an automatic control system.
Wallace compares it to the centrifugal governor of a steam engine. That device keeps the engine stable by detecting deviations and correcting them early, before they become large.
In biological terms, the idea is:
organisms vary,
some variations help survival,
some harm survival,
harmful variations are “corrected” because their bearers survive less well and reproduce less,
so, the population is kept adaptively tuned to the environment.

Therefore, the first conceptual principle is:

"Deviation from adaptive fit"→"survival cost"→"correction by selection"

This is already a feedback model.

# Consciousness evolved  as natural selection’s intelligent survival mechanism in biological systems from the very beginning of life; without it, 

# no life.

## 2. Why this suggests a Bayesian interpretation

A Bayesian system also works by correction through evidence.

In Bayesian inference:

you begin with a prior expectation,

you encounter evidence,

you update your confidence,

weak hypotheses lose probability,

strong hypotheses gain probability.

So both systems have the same abstract structure:

Control-system language

deviation is detected,

error is corrected.

Bayesian language

mismatch is detected,

probability is updated.

This lets us reinterpret Wallace’s idea:

A trait is like a hypothesis about how to survive in a given world.
The environment tests that hypothesis.
Survival and reproduction supply the evidence.
The population then updates accordingly.

## That is the bridge from natural selection to Bayesian selection.

### Step 1: trait as hypothesis

Let 𝑇_𝑖 be a trait, phenotype, or survival strategy.

We now interpret:

𝑇_𝑖="a biological hypothesis about viable living"

Examples:

thicker fur = hypothesis for cold survival

camouflage = hypothesis for predator avoidance

social bonding tendency = hypothesis for group survival

pain sensitivity = hypothesis for bodily protection

Not all hypotheses are equally good.

Some fit the environment better than others.

### Step 2: environment as evidence

Let E denote the environmental conditions:

E="the survival test imposed by reality" 

This includes:
predators,
climate,
scarcity,
disease,
competition,
social pressures,
mating conditions.

The environment, therefore, is the source of evidence used to evaluate traits.

### Step 3: prior probability of a trait

Before selection occurs, the trait T_ihas some existing frequency in the population:

P_t (T_i)

This is its prior probability at generation t.

In plain English:

How common is this trait already?

How much weight does it currently have in the population?

### Step 4: fitness as likelihood

Now ask:

If an organism has trait T_i, how likely is it to survive and reproduce under environment E?

That gives the likelihood term:

P(S∣T_i,E)

where Smeans survival-reproductive success.

This is the evolutionary version of Bayesian likelihood.

So:

if a trait performs well, likelihood is high

if it performs badly, likelihood is low

This is the formal version of Wallace’s “correction” mechanism.

### Step 5: posterior updating by selection

Bayes’ rule gives:
P_(t+1) (T_i)=P(T_i∣S,E)∝P(S∣T_i,E)" " P_t (T_i)

Normalising across all traits:
P_(t+1) (T_i)=(P(S∣T_i,E)" " P_t (T_i))/(∑_j〖P(S∣〗 T_j,E)" " P_t (T_j))

This is the Bayesian Framework of Selection.

Meaning

The next-generation frequency of a trait depends on:
how common it already was,

multiplied by how strongly survival evidence supports it.
So natural selection becomes:

#### population-level Bayesian updating across generations.

### Step 6: Wallace’s automatic correction in Bayesian form

Now we can restate Wallace precisely.

If a trait is maladaptive, then:
P(S∣T_i,E)
is low.

Therefore:
P_(t+1) (T_i)<P_t (T_i)

So the trait is updated downward quickly.

This is exactly the meaning of Wallace’s claim that deficiencies do not grow into large conspicuous imbalances. They are checked early because reality immediately penalises them.

Therfore, Wallace’s control-system metaphor becomes:
"maladaptive trait"→"negative evidence"→"downward Bayesian update" 

### Step 7: from selection across generations to inference within a lifetime

So far, updates have been applied across generations.

But now we take the next step.

If evolution works by Bayesian correction across generations, then organisms within a lifetime may also need to perform a faster version of the same logic.

Why?

Because survival cannot wait only for long-term selection.

The organism must respond now to:
danger,
hunger,
injury,
opportunity,
social threat,
reproductive signals. Etc.

Therefore, there are two levels:

### Evolutionary timescale

#### Traits are updated across generations.

### Organismic timescale

#### Perceptions, expectations, and actions, etc., are updated moment by moment.

### Step 8: real-time Bayesian inference inside the organism

Let H_kbe a hypothesis about some current state of the world or body.

Examples:

“that shape is a snake”,
“I am dehydrated”,
“that person is angry”,
“food is available”,
“injury is occurring”

The organism receives current data D_tand updates:

P(H_k∣D_t)∝P(D_t∣H_k) P(H_k)

This is ordinary Bayesian inference.

Now the structure of life becomes parallel at two scales:

Evolution

P_(t+1) (T_i)∝P(S∣T_i,E) P_t (T_i)

Perception/action

P(H_k∣D_t)∝P(D_t∣H_k) P(H_k)

#### Evolution updates traits across generations.

#### The organism updates interpretations within the present moment.

### Step 9: Why AST is needed

Why are survival-relevant inferences felt?

Why are they conscious as pain, fear, comfort, hunger, longing, relief, hope, etc?

AST’s answer is:

Consciousness evolved because survival-relevant uncertainty, need, value, and error had to become experientially salient to the organism.

In other words:
It is not enough to register signals mechanically,
The organism must feel its significance,
because feeling prioritises action.

So, AST adds affective phenomenology to the inferential framework.
