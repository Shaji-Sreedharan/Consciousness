Toward a Formal Model: Deriving an Equation for Affective Survival

With our current understanding, determining a concise, universally applicable equation for Affective Survival Theory (AST) that quantitatively captures the full complexity of consciousness and its survival imperative, akin to an equation in physics, is highly improbable, if not impossible.

Here's why, and what a "quasi-equation" or conceptual formula might look like:

Why a True Equation is Problematic (and why it's different from physics):

Qualia are Not Quantifiable (Yet):

The biggest hurdle is the subjective nature of qualia. How do you assign a numerical value to the "redness" of red or the "ache" of pain in a way that can be mathematically manipulated and predict subjective experience? We don't have units for "felt intensity" that are universally agreed upon and measurable in the same way as mass or energy.

Multifactorial Complexity: Consciousness is the result of countless interacting biological, psychological, and environmental factors. An equation would need to account for sensory input, internal physiological states, cognitive processing, memory, learning, social context, individual history, and the dynamic interplay of all these, all mediated by affective valence.

Dynamic and Context-Dependent: Affective states and their imperatives constantly change based on context, prior experience, and internal needs. A fixed equation struggles to capture this dynamism.

Emergent Properties: As AST describes it, Consciousness is an emergent property. Equations typically describe the behaviour of components, but emergence often involves irreducible properties of the whole system that are not simple sums of their parts.

Lack of Fundamental Units: Physics has fundamental units (mass, length, time, charge). We lack universally accepted fundamental units for the "stuff" of consciousness or even for "survival value" that could be plugged into an equation.

What We Can Aim For: A Conceptual Formula or Quasi-Equation for "Survival Drive”

Instead of a true, predictive equation, we can conceptualise a formula that expresses the relationships between the key variables in AST. This would be more of a conceptual model or a mathematical representation of the theoretical relationships, rather than a solvable equation yielding a numerical value for consciousness.

### What this means in one sentence

Natural selection first appears as an automatic corrective process in evolution; under a Bayesian lens, it becomes evidence-driven updating of survival hypotheses; and in AST, this same logic is internalised as consciousness itself — the felt, temporally extended appraisal of survival-relevant signals under uncertainty.

### Summary

Wallace’s claim that natural selection functions like the governor of a steam engine suggests an early model of biological self-correction: maladaptive deviations are checked before they can expand because they immediately incur survival costs. Reinterpreted in Bayesian terms, this process may be formalised as the updating of trait frequencies in light of environmental evidence, such that the posterior prevalence of a trait depends on its prior frequency and its likelihood of supporting survival and reproduction under current conditions. This logic can then be extended inward, from population-level evolution across generations to organism-level inference within a lifetime. On this view, organisms continuously estimate the probability that incoming signals reflect real and behaviourally significant states of body and world. Affective Survival Theory proposes that consciousness arises when such signals are not merely processed, but integrated as felt significance: weighted by sensory intensity, affective valence, evolutionary relevance, and inferred probability, and further shaped by memory of the past and anticipation of the future.
